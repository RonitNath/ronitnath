"Also the current calendar product is not good. As a human, I click around, and it doesn't work the way I expect. (new visit doesn't work, t doesn't move the calendar, can't confirm dragndrop, etc)"

Settles the quality verdict on the one existing screen — and **qualifies EV-020**, which judged
the schedule desk polished. The divergence is instructive: EV-020's assessment came from
accessibility-tree-driven automation (clicks on named roles), which succeeded; a human driving
mouse and keyboard hits failures — New visit not working, the `T` shortcut not moving the
calendar, drag-and-drop unverifiable. Ground truth outranks inference: the calendar does not
yet meet the bar a human holds it to, and automation-passes-but-human-fails is itself a finding
about how this product must be validated (machine walkthroughs are insufficient evidence of UX
correctness; cf. EV-024's premium-experience requirement). The calendar is foundation, not
finished work.
