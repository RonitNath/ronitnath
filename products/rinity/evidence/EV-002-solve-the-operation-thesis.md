"Long-term thesis: 'we will solve this business operation for you.' Expand the set of business-operation capabilities Isoastra can absorb until the offer spans anything from a disjoint set of single operations up to running the whole business machine — while the owner focuses on whatever drives value in their industry (for dentists: patient care) and retains sufficient control over how their business runs and their personal business philosophy."

Settles what rinity is a first instance of: owning the client-service interface of a practice's
phone lines is one absorbed business operation, not the product ceiling. Two consequences bind
design: (1) the architecture must leave room for absorbing adjacent operations per vertical
(e.g. automated insurance checks in dental); (2) the practice owner's control over how their
business runs is a first-class requirement, not a settings page — the owner's business philosophy
must be expressible in how the agent behaves.
