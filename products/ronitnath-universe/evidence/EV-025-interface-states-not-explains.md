"There are a variety of explanatory quips scattered around which I don't like. Redesign focusing on
minimalism, and don't explain so much. Examples: 'Ronit already knows', 'Saves carry the value's
revision — a stale save is rejected, never silently applied.' 'Invites can still be minted; they'll
start working the moment you publish.' 'Becomes a contact — this is how the directory learns about
people organically.' 'Confirm the yeses, correct the handful that are wrong' 'updating as people
answer' and the '• Live', 'event-specific — no other event has this'. These are part of a broader
pattern of AI built interface preference I greatly dislike. The visual hierarchy due to text sizing
is also annoying. the 'Hey Shaz' style for the personalization reads as an eyebrow. I hate eyebrows.
THe visual weight difference between the title of the event all the other event content is also
annoying. At most, it ought to be a little larger, and not heavier in weight."

Four rulings in one message, all about how a surface reads rather than what it covers. They are
recorded together because the owner's own framing — "a broader pattern" — is the point: individually
they are eight strings, collectively they are a house style being rejected. Formalized as DEC-012.

1. **The interface states; it does not explain.** Every named string justifies the design, reassures
   the reader, or narrates what just happened. None of them tells the owner anything about his data.
2. **Type hierarchy was doing work that colour and space should do.** The file carried nineteen sizes.
3. **No eyebrows** — no small label stacked above a bigger thing, including the greeting that opened
   the invite page.
4. **Larger, never heavier.** A title earns its place by size and typeface, not weight.

The provenance matters here more than usual. This is not a preference an agent could have derived
from the design brief — `docs/design.md` is compatible with the rejected file — and it is the kind of
ruling that will be silently violated by the next generated surface unless it is written down as a
constraint rather than remembered as a review comment.
